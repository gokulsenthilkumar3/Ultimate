Why one tape reading isn’t enough — width, depth and girth

Two people can read the same number on a tape and still be shaped differently. Girth is all the way around; width is straight across; depth is front to back. This slice is cut from the 3D model at the measurement you picked, seen from above.

The tape stays fixed at 80 cm all the way round while the shape changes (ellipse maths, Ramanujan’s approximation). A real torso isn’t a perfect oval — compare with the slice above.

Words used on this page

Girth (circumference)
A complete loop around a body part. Not a width, and not a diameter.
Width / breadth
A straight line from one side to the other. Shoulder width across the bony tips differs from the widest point of the shoulder muscles.
Depth
Front to back thickness at one level, for example chest depth.
Projection
How far a shape sticks out from a named flat reference, such as the seat behind a line dropped from the hip joint.
Landmark
A spot you can find by touch, such as the top edge of the hip bone (iliac crest) or the bony tip of the shoulder (acromion).
Soft tape
A flexible measuring tape. Snug, level, not squeezing the skin.
Fitted / Saved only
“Fitted” means the model was adjusted until it measures what you typed. “Saved only” means the number is kept, but the preview doesn’t use it yet.
Optional · private measurements (adults only)

These belong to the private anatomy area of the app, behind its own adult and privacy controls. They are not drawn on this page’s model. The schematic below only explains how “base”, “middle” and “distal” loops are defined: a loop at a named section, perpendicular to the part’s own centre line.

  • Base = near where it attaches to the body. Distal = farther from the attachment. A loop is a full circle of tape, not a straight width.
  • Record the state (at rest or erect) and the section together. Leave anything unclear as “not recorded”.
  • Entry pattern for these fields: a plain name, a diagram, “Skip”, and a clear “Saved only / shown in preview” label.

Today: eight tape sites fit to the model. Next: shoulders, torso and limbs as separate width, depth and contour controls.
2 · Surface
Today: a photo-based colour map, one global colour, one regional pigment, one tan layer. Next: pores and fine wrinkles (normal maps), roughness variation, light scattering under the skin.
3 · Face and eyes
Today: generic. Next: eyelid contact, lips, ears, hairline; a guided visual editor instead of dozens of face measurements.
4 · Hands and feet
Today: generic proportions, fixed pose. Next: finger and toe proportions, nails, arch.

Today: a fixed A-pose, on purpose, so measurements are comparable. Next: seated, bent and raised poses with joint corrections.

What every field must show

A friendly entry

  • A plain name and a one-sentence meaning
  • Where, in words you can find by touch
  • The tool, the pose, and one common mistake
  • Units you choose; typical range as a hint, never a wall
  • “Skip”, “Adjust by eye” and “Enter a number” as separate routes

An honest result

  • What the model measures now
  • Fitted · Model limit · Roughly shown · Saved only
  • Where the number came from: tape, helper, scan, estimate
  • Nothing sensitive in screenshots or analytics
  • Keyboard and screen-reader equivalents for every diagram

First implementation: height, protocol-specific waist, hips, shoulder width, chest depth, and base / middle / distal local sections.

✓

Evidence instead of “100%”

Proposed acceptance targets — freeze them after repeatability and device measurements.

  • Extraction: known circles, ovals and boxes return the right length, perimeter and area; open meshes give no volume.
  • Fitting: length / width / depth within max(0.5 cm, 1%); girth within max(1 cm, 1%) on synthetic bodies. This tests the solver, not a scan.
  • Repeatability: repeated real measurements; report median and 95th-percentile error and bias per measurement.
  • Likeness: match calibrated front / side / back outlines where references exist; say which regions are missing.
  • Appearance: fixed renders, two lights, rated per layer — never one realism percentage.
  • Usability: ≥5 first-time users; ≥80% find each tested site and tool unaided.
  • Performance: p95 frame time ≤ 33 ms on a mid-tier phone and ≤ 16.7 ms on the chosen desktop, at stated resolution.
  • Regression: cm ↔ in round-trips, blank stays blank, imported method stays unknown, current and goal never mix.

About this preview
