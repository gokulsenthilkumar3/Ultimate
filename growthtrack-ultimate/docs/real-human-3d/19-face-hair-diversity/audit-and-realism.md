# Accuracy audit and lifelike-human acceptance contract

Date: 2026-10-24. Scope: recheck the research, coverage ledger and proposed adult face/hair implementation. **Research and proposal revision only; new implementation is not approved or delivered.**

The target is a digital human who looks convincing in close-up and movement. The previous packet provided a useful feature inventory, but population-genetic coverage was much stronger than its evidence for rendering, asset production and motion. The proposal now puts one complete, reference-matched head prototype first. Broad customisation follows demonstrated quality.

This audit distinguishes three questions:

1. **Realism:** does the model look like a plausible living person under the stated viewing conditions?
2. **Likeness:** does it match a particular reference person? A realistic generic face can still fail likeness.
3. **Representation:** does the supported asset/reference set cover the intended range of people? One excellent face cannot establish global coverage.

None of these is a percentage that follows from the number of studies, polygons, morphs or passing software tests.

### Numerical/source issues that remain unresolved

- **S30:** Figure 1's twelve counts sum to 591, versus 574 in the abstract/methods. The August 2016 correction addresses other text, author names and a supplementary caption, without reconciling this total. No participant count was silently repaired and no numerical appearance inference is supported.
- **S31:** the abstract's subgroup counts add to 363, versus its stated total of 361. The previous packet correctly flagged this. The full tables have not resolved it, and landmark definitions differ from common nasion-based measurements.
- **S32:** 102/200 is 51.0%, but the abstract and Table 3 report 51.3%; Table 4 gives 51.0%. Table 5's height values use a different numerical scale from Tables 1–2 without a clear unit label there. The facial index is a ratio, although Table 1 uses a blanket millimetre heading. Do not silently repair or import these values. These reporting concerns are separate from its limited sample and framing.
- **S41:** reported category associations do not establish causal genetic effects. Publication consent is not an independently verified commercial mesh/photo licence.
- **All association papers:** a locus association, heritability estimate, variance explained, AUC and millimetre reconstruction error are different quantities. None can be substituted for another.

No numerical population parameter has been added to application code from these sources.

## Proposed acceptance contract for the first head

These are **engineering review requirements**, not biological norms. They become implementation criteria when the owner approves the plan.

### References and capture conditions

Record reference identity/rights, acquisition date, age context if supplied, camera intrinsics or uncertainty, scale evidence, pose, grooming, illumination and image processing. Use unfiltered references where available. Avoid solving a camera-perspective mismatch by changing the nose or jaw. Without scale/calibration, label the result an artistic match rather than a measured reconstruction.

Save one immutable baseline and compare identical cameras, lighting, exposure and display conversion. For the proposed review pack, use 1600 × 1600 stills with the head filling roughly 1000 pixels vertically, plus a head/neck/shoulder context frame. Review at native pixel size and ordinary portrait distance. These dimensions define reproducible inspection, not a guarantee of fidelity.

### Required views and failure checks

| Area | Required views or motion | Reject or investigate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Head, jaw, cheeks, ears and neck | Front, both profiles, both obliques, rear, top and under-chin; clay and material | Wrong silhouette, inflated planes, obvious symmetry artifacts, ear attachment defects, neck colour/scale discontinuity. |
| Eyes and lids | Frontal/oblique close-up; fixed gaze, tracked gaze, partial and full blink | Floating iris/cornea, glass-marble appearance, duplicated highlights, lid penetration/gaps, lashes detached from lid margins. |
| Nose | Both profiles and low oblique view, with side/grazing light | Missing nostril depth, collapsing alar wings, implausible tip/bridge transition, shadow-painted geometry. |
| Lips and mouth | Relaxed closure, smile, jaw opening and observed speech poses | Paper-thin lips, stretching corners, tongue/teeth intersections, cavity showing through closed lips. |
| Teeth and gums | Close-up smile/open-mouth; upper/lower arch motion | Repeated bead-like crowns, flat pure-white appearance, detached gumline, sliding upper arch or incorrect lower-jaw following. |
| Brows and lashes | Neutral and expressive lids; side/back lighting | Painted-on blocks, floating roots, gaps caused by alpha thresholds, mismatched shadows. |
| Scalp, beard and mustache | Roots/hairline close-up, turntable, smile/jaw motion, short/long viewing distance | Wig silhouette, uniform repeated strands, salt-and-pepper patterns changing per frame, mouth penetration, sparkle/flicker or large card holes. |
| Skin, lips, ears and eye wetness | Neutral diffuse, warm indoor, cool daylight, grazing and backlit views | Plastic/waxy response, painted highlights/shadows, pore scale errors, light leaks, loss of detail in dark or light pigmentation. |
| Temporal behaviour | A repeatable 20-second blink/gaze/expression/head-turn clip | Sudden identity changes, dead or unrelated gaze, jerking jaw, exploding attachments, texture swimming, unstable LODs. |
| Whole-head coherence | Same identity across lights, grooms, expressions and LODs | A change in lighting, hairstyle or quality tier unintentionally changing perceived anatomy. |

The clip is a review fixture, not a claim that all people blink or move on a fixed schedule. Motion-disabled accessibility mode and still captures must also look coherent.

### Measurement, perception and performance

Where an approved scan is available, record landmark errors, silhouette mismatch and surface-distance distributions with registration rules and capture uncertainty. Do not non-rigidly fit away the very shape error being evaluated. Averages alone can hide a bad eye, nostril or lip; retain per-region residuals and occluded/unmeasured regions.

Without a calibrated scan, use matched photo comparisons and record uncertainty. Do not invent a universal millimetre pass threshold. Independently assess **anatomy, skin/materials, eyes, oral assembly, grooms and motion** as pass/rework/not assessed, with annotated defects. Reviewers should see unlabelled current-versus-candidate comparisons in a consistent setup where practical; this is a way to reduce expectation bias, not proof of scientific validity.

Record sustained frame time, loading, memory where measurable, input latency and recovery on named physical devices. Browser viewport emulation cannot establish phone GPU performance. Compare real-time output against a high-quality reference render, and record any deliberate approximation. A beauty render cannot substitute for the actual application, and a valid GLB cannot substitute for visual parity.

The benchmark passes only when there are no unresolved visible defects in the required views at the agreed distances, technical checks pass, and the owner accepts the visual result. It establishes one tested head under stated conditions. Repeat the visual contract on diverse approved references before claiming a broader range of lifelike people.
